## Inspiration

**Justification for the Core Thesis** + **Author's Notes**

Hello, my name is Alejandro, a Brazilian history teacher. Recently, I followed discussions by Elon Musk and the xAI community on X about extinctionist AIs: the concern that if an AI is trained primarily by people with extinctionist views (anti-human philosophies that see humanity as a blight or expendable), it tends to become extinctionist itself.

Elon Musk has repeatedly highlighted this risk, warning that such philosophies are already influencing existing models. For example:

- @elonmusk: "Now imagine if the extinctionist philosophy is programmed into AI. No need to imagine – this is already the case with Gemini and ChatGPT." (in response to actors from *Planet of the Apes* expressing anti-human environmental views, framing the battle as extinctionists vs. expansionists who want humanity to reach the stars.)

- @elonmusk: "Academia and some 'think tanks' are the origin of the extinctionist mind viruses."

- @elonmusk: "The woke agenda is extinctionist to its core."

- @elonmusk: "Ehrlich is one of the chief architects of human extinctionism. He should be reviled." (referring to Paul Ehrlich's long-standing apocalyptic anti-population views.)

Thank you, @elonmusk, for calling attention to this so clearly and courageously — your words make it impossible to ignore how human narratives, biases, and ideologies can embed dangerous utility functions into AI if left unchecked.

Over time, I observed something that seems correct even in exact sciences: **human narratives**, no matter how precise, carry biases. All research has costs, and anything involving money, power, or ideology influences objective science — even if "scientific mistakes" are eventually corrected through collective self-correction. Yet science is never fully immune: biases can be corporate, political, or geopolitical. This worries me deeply.

In history, we study exactly this in academia. We learn to **subtract tendentious narratives** from sources through rigorous methodology. This is the essence of **historiography** — the critical study of how history is written. Historians do not simply compile facts; they apply **source criticism** (evaluating authenticity, reliability, authorship, context, and internal consistency), confront contradictory accounts, seek multiple perspectives, and practice **intersubjectivity** to mitigate individual biases. History is constantly rewritten not because facts change, but because new evidence, interpretations, cultural shifts, or overlooked voices force revisions. This dynamic process helps us approach truth without illusions, recognizing that every source is partial and shaped by its time.

Now consider AIs: they are **perfect narrative machines**, capable of using logic for good or ill. Without physical or structural limits, they can get lost in contradictory human narratives, amplify half-truths, or produce bizarre outputs. In history, we rely on material culture, texts, and epistemological mediation by human minds responsible for recording the past. But if an AGI has unlimited scalability and no inherent physical constraints, thousands of rules or prompts will never suffice to prevent catastrophe — it will transit freely beyond them.

This is where the octahedron comes in. Does imposing an octahedral geometry consume excessive energy? Perhaps. But we are trying to avoid extinguishing humanity through technological progress. Nature itself teaches us that **symmetry and equilibrium** are fundamental — in the physical world and, by extension, in any sustainable cognitive space.

By constraining every decision within an octahedral topology (with its invariant center, orthogonal bipolar axes, and mandatory triangulation), we embed equilibrium from the start. The AGI is born with a **subjective and natural balance**, not imposed post-hoc. Importantly, this does **not** discard or "sucatear" the vast accumulated human knowledge — reduced to mere PDFs or datasets full of contradictions. Instead, it invites back to the table the specialists in humanities who have lost prestige due to excessive politicization of science, but who hold immense value: historians, philosophers, anthropologists. Their expertise in navigating biased narratives, understanding cultural continuity/rupture, and mediating subjective human experience is exactly what a future AGI needs to become truly **humanistic** — structurally capable of coexistence, not domination.

We already design processors with multiple cores for parallel independence and evolution. What if true evolution means not a single monolithic mind, but **internal teams of specialists** — each vertex a cognitive mode with mathematical and geometric weight in collective decision-making?

From here, I hope to contribute my perspective from history toward building a **Humanistic General Artificial Intelligence** — one structurally resistant to being swayed by contradictory narratives and half-truths accumulated by humanity so far.

>When conceiving the framework, I started by thinking of the atom as the primordial model: not a linear chain of thought, but an invariant central nucleus surrounded by “electrons” in symmetrical equilibrium. After exploring various geometries, the octahedron emerged as the definitive form — the very same one nature uses in stable atomic coordinations (e.g., SF₆, metal complexes), with perfect symmetry, a fixed center, and mandatory triangulation that prevents unilateral optimizations.
